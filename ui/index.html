RESEARCH QUESTION • ROUTING & NOISE

How Chip Geometry Affects Quantum Circuit Fidelity

We study how qubit connectivity changes routing overhead, hardware-noise exposure, and the fidelity of quantum states under a simulated Qiskit Aer transmon noise model.

PROBLEM

Distant qubits require additional routing operations.

Quantum processor layouts cannot connect every qubit pair directly. When two qubits need to share an entangled state, they must communicate across intermediate physical hops.

MECHANISM

Routing adds SWAP gates and circuit depth, increasing noise exposure.

Every compiler-inserted SWAP operation expands into three physical CX gates, increasing execution duration and exposing the circuit to gate errors and decoherence.

SOLUTION STUDIED

Stabilizer-based error detection with post-selection filters corrupted runs.

Helper ancilla qubits check bit-flip and phase-flip parities during simulation. Corrupted runs are discarded, recovering fidelity on the retained results.

📖 What does this mean? Plain-English Glossary
Click to expand / collapse
Routing

The extra operations needed to move quantum information between qubits that do not have a direct physical connection.

SWAP

A circuit operation that exchanges quantum states between two adjacent connected physical qubits.

CX Gate

The standard Controlled-NOT two-qubit gate used by the quantum processor to generate quantum entanglement.

Noise

Unwanted physical hardware imperfections (gate infidelity, decoherence, measurement errors) that degrade quantum states.

Fidelity

How closely the measured quantum state matches the theoretical target state. 1.0000 denotes a perfect match.

Stabilizer

A quantum parity check used to detect whether bit-flip or phase-flip errors occurred during execution.

Post-Selection

Keeping only the experimental runs that passed all error checks, discarding any corrupted measurements.

ERROR MITIGATION

How Protection Works: Simple 4-Step Process

We use stabilizer-based error detection to identify corrupted runs, and post-selection to remove them from analysis.

1

Run Noisy Circuit

Execute the entangled Bell-state circuit under simulated superconducting transmon noise.

→
2

Measure Stabilizer Checks

Helper ancilla qubits perform parity checks to detect bit-flip (ZZ) and phase-flip (XX) errors.

→
3

Reject Runs with Errors

Discard failed runs where syndrome ≠ 00, filtering out detected errors.

→
4

Calculate Fidelity on Accepted Runs

The retained uncorrupted runs exhibit significantly higher measured state fidelity.

⚠

Crucial Operational Principle: Protection does not repair a corrupted run. It identifies runs likely affected by detectable errors and removes them from the final analysis.

Corrupted runs are detected and discarded during offline data filtering rather than actively corrected using real-time quantum feedforward operations.

Best for scalable planar lattices with error mitigation
Benchmark: 0.8327 → 0.9255 (+11.15%) • 72.37% kept

  • Routing depth can be absorbed or bridged through stabilizers

Finite Hyperbolic (21Q)

Best for balanced interconnects • Low diameter
Benchmark: 0.8871 → 0.9146 (+3.09%) • 78.59% kept
  • Low graph diameter (4 hops) and uniform central hub connectivity are valued
  • Balanced shot survival (~79%) with moderate fidelity recovery (+3.09%)
  • Future non-planar or 3D multichip interconnects
Decision Principle: No single architecture is universally best. Heavy-Hex has the highest protected fidelity (0.9255) and largest measured improvement (+11.15%), but Star/T retains the highest percentage of experimental runs (85.65%). Therefore, the best architecture depends on the routing objective, target connection, and available shot budget.

10 Questions Every Visitor Can Answer

Click any question to view its plain-English explanation:

1. What problem is this project solving?

When two qubits are far apart on a chip, the computer must insert extra operations to connect them. These extra operations introduce noise and lower the reliability of the quantum result. We study how chip geometry influences this.

2. What are the three architectures being compared?

1) Star/T (5Q): small, hub-based layout; 2) Heavy-Hex (27Q): sparse, grid-like layout; 3) Finite Hyperbolic (21Q): high-connectivity experimental layout.

3. What does "routing" mean in this project?

The extra operations (SWAPs) needed to move quantum information between qubits that are not directly connected on the chip.

4. Why does routing matter?

Every SWAP operation expands into native gates. More gates increase run-time and expose data qubits to hardware errors.

5. What happens when noise is introduced?

Physical hardware noise degrades the quality of the state, causing the fidelity to drop from the ideal 1.0000 down to between 0.83 and 0.90.

6. What does "fidelity" mean?

How closely the measured quantum state matches the intended state. 1.0000 is a perfect match.

7. What does the protection step do?

It uses helper ancilla qubits to perform stabilizer parity checks, detecting whether bit-flip or phase-flip errors occurred during execution.

8. Why does protection improve the measured result?

Through post-selection, runs that failed the error checks are discarded, leaving only clean, uncorrupted quantum states.

9. What is the cost of protection?

Discarding runs reduces the usable shot survival yield (up to 27.6% of runs were discarded on Heavy-Hex), and protection requires extra gates.

10. What was the main finding of the experiment?

Error detection improved fidelity across all three architectures. Heavy-Hex showed the largest relative gain (+11.15%, 0.8327 → 0.9255), but also discarded the most experimental runs (27.63% discarded).
